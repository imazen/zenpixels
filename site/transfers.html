Why pixel codes are curved, not linear

Human eyes see brightness roughly logarithmically: a tiny step near black looks as big as a huge jump near white. Transfer functions bend stored codes to match human vision (and HDR headroom). Hover the charts to inspect exact EOTF/OETF values, compare bit allocation, and generate live zenpixels-convert Rust code below.

Look at signal = 0.50 (code 128/255): In Linear, 0.50 is 50% light (already bright gray). In sRGB, 0.50 is 21.4% light — spending half of all 8-bit codes on darks below 21%. In PQ, 0.50 is 92.2 nits out of 10,000 (just 0.92% of peak!) — spending over half its codes below SDR white so shadows never band.
