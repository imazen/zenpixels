# Power-of-two row strides: aliasing cost per SIMD tier (2026-11-01)

Question: does a row stride that is a multiple of 4096 bytes slow kernels that read
one column across several rows, and does adding 64 bytes fix it? The zenpixels docs
carried "add 64 bytes at a multiple of 4096" from rav1d (which adds 64 at a multiple
of 1024) without a measurement.

Bench: `zenpixels-convert/benches/bench_stride_aliasing.rs` (tree `dc3bf1e9`, bench
unchanged since), zenbench 0.1.11, `--no-busy-gate`, 20 fixed rounds per group, every
tier called directly with its token. Before timing, every tier and stride is checked
bit-identical to a scalar reference (passed on both hosts).

```sh
cargo bench -p zenpixels-convert --features avx512 --bench bench_stride_aliasing -- --no-busy-gate   # x86
cargo bench -p zenpixels-convert --bench bench_stride_aliasing -- --no-busy-gate                     # aarch64
python3 -I benchmarks/stride_aliasing_tables.py LOG1 LOG2 --rows ROWLOG... > tables.md               # the tables below
```

## What is measured

1. **Vertical K-tap filter**, K = 2, 8, 12: `out[y][x] = sum_k w[k] * in[y+k][x]` on F32,
   16-float column blocks, the K input rows live together, tightly packed output. Each group
   compares a stride with the same row width (stride/4 floats) at stride + 64 bytes. Strides
   in bytes: 1024, 2048, 4096, 6144, 8192, 12288, 16384, 32768 (6144 is a multiple of 2048
   but not 4096; 12288 a multiple of 4096 but not a power of two). Rows start 64-byte aligned.
   L2 case: about 128 KiB of input (at least K+4 rows). Memory case: 128 MiB of input.
   Output buffer at page offset 2048 (`out@2048`) or 0 (`out@0`, where a store can share low
   12 address bits with nearby loads); input at page offset 0.
2. **Row in, row out**: `dst[y] = src[y] * g + b`, `dst` in the same allocation 4096n + {64, 0,
   -64, 2048} bytes after `src`; +64 is the baseline. 1024 floats per row, stride 4160 B; 2 rows
   (L1), 32 rows (L2), 16384 rows (memory).

Cells: zenbench's 95% CI of the power-of-two stride's time against its +64 partner, in
percent (`+30` = the power-of-two stride is 30% slower). Intervals are within one process;
**between processes results disagree more than the intervals say**, so each cell shows two
full runs (r1, r2) and `*` marks cells whose midpoints differ by over 5 points. The memory
case is the least stable.

## Reading (interpretation unless marked measured)

Measured, Zen 4, L2-resident rows (both runs, all tiers, both output placements):

- **K=2: no effect.** Every interval at every stride lies within -5.2..+5.8%.
- **K=8: only 4096, +4.3..+11.5%.** Other strides -3.1..+9.5%, tier medians at most 3.4%.
- **K=12: multiples of 4096 cost 19-48% from 8192 up and 4-19% at 4096.** 8192, 12288, 16384,
  32768: +18.7..+47.9% (medians of interval midpoints: scalar about 21%, AVX2 32%, AVX-512
  32-35%). 4096: +3.5..+19.1% (medians scalar 16, AVX2 10, AVX-512 6.5). 1024, 2048, 6144:
  -6.4..+2.2%, no loss.
- **The trigger is a multiple of 4096**, not a power of two (12288 behaves like 8192) and not
  a multiple of 1024 or 2048 (1024, 2048 and 6144 show nothing). These cache-resident
  measurements do not support rav1d's 1024 threshold on Zen 4.
- **+64 recovers all of it** (it is the baseline). Other paddings were not compared.
- Fits an 8-way 32 KiB L1D set conflict past 8 rows at one column (hypothesis; no counters read).
- `out@0` is a few points worse than `out@2048` at 4096 (v4, K=12, L2: +3.5..+5.1% against
  +7.4..+15.0%), same pattern. Before the buffers had pinned page offsets, the same binary gave
  +22% and +4% for that cell in two runs: allocator placement alone moved it.

Measured, Zen 4, memory-resident rows: unstable between runs and tiers.
- K=12 at 8192-32768: +6.6..+42.2% in every cell; at 4096: -2.9..+41.9%.
- K=2 is hit too at 12288 and 16384 (+7..+29% and +1..+21% across tiers), unlike L2-resident rows.
- K=8 is hit at 2048 and 6144 too (medians +8..+65% and +10..+34%, scalar worst), so the 4096
  rule does not predict memory-resident behaviour. Not explained.
- At 1024 the power-of-two stride is faster than +64 for K=2 and K=8 (medians -3..-11%): the
  bump costs where it is not needed.

Measured, Neoverse-N1:
- **Almost no effect.** L2-resident: the power-of-two stride is never more than 4.0% slower,
  except K=12 at 8192 (+6.4..+9.7%); at 4096, 12288, 16384 and 32768, K=12 is within
  -2.0..+1.8%. N1's 64 KiB 4-way L1D would predict conflicts at 16384 and 32768 as well; none
  appeared (not explained). K=2 at 32768 is 4-15% faster without the bump.
- Memory-resident: K=8 at 2048 is 5-22% faster without the bump; K=12 at 8192 is +4.7..+8.7%
  slower; at 4096 the bump costs up to 7% (K=2, K=8).

Row in, row out (4K load/store aliasing):
- **+64 is not a fix; on Zen 4 AVX-512 it was the slow placement.** v4, L2: the other three
  placements took 26-36% less time than 4096n+64 in 3 of 4 process runs and were equal to it in
  the fourth (baseline 2.1-2.4 us against 1.5 us). Those three are indistinguishable from each
  other in every run. Reading (untested): with dst 64 bytes past a 4096 multiple, the next
  vector's load matches the low 12 address bits of the previous vector's pending store.
- Everything else is within about +-4%: AVX2 -3.5..+3.7%, scalar -4.1..+0.3%, v4 memory
  -2.6..+2.9% on x86; NEON and scalar on N1 -3.2..+3.6%.
- L1 (2 rows) is noise: in run 1 the scalar baseline took 159 ns against 94-99 ns in the others.
- Two earlier full runs (same row kernel, before 6144 and 12288 were added) gave -22%.

## Not measured

u8/u16 samples; more than 12 live rows; heavier arithmetic; paddings other than +64; transposes and 8x8 blocks; hardware counters;
Zen 5, Intel, Apple silicon, wasm128; strides above 32768; L1-resident vertical filters.
